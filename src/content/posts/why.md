> > > > WHY?
> > > > 
> > > > questionnaire from [berlinbeat.org](http://berlinbeat.org/)
> > > > 
> > > > \------------------------------
> > > > 
> > > > This fall has been busy for the group, with some real intense touring happening. On that note, how is tour going? What have been some of the more memorable locations and is there anything in particular you are looking forward to in the coming month? After the holidays, what does your 2013 touring schedule look like?
> > > > 
> > > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > We are holding up surprisingly well considering we've been out since August and barely have any days off. I really like the quaint little European towns. I like to wander around and try to get lost. St. Gallen Switzerland was cute as hell. Looks like Epcot Center.
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > >   
> > > > 
> > > > By the way, the photos from each show are an awesome idea-what inspired that?
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > That was Sarah Winters' (our piano player) idea.
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > >   
> > > > 
> > > > Berlin is obviously an important place for the band--not only does the city turn up in lyrics but you have a label there as well. Since I write about Berlin music, can you tell me a little more about this connection, elaborating on what your observations, thoughts, or experiences with the city and its music scene are?
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > Well we have spent a lot of time there since our booking agent and now our label is there. We spent the summer of '08 there. Summer in Berlin is the absolute best. All the parks and beer gardens, outdoor kinos, etc.
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > >   
> > > > 
> > > > What other places are close to you? There is Cincinnati obviously, the East Bay... are there other places? Do you associate various places with certain stages of your musical development?
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > Definitely Cincy and Oakland/Berkeley. I've also spent a bit of time in New York and like it there. This little town called Yellow Springs in Ohio, I spent a month working on music up there once.
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > And yes, I do associate certain periods of work with certain places.
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > >   
> > > > 
> > > > So the new music-excellent stuff! Unique but still very much the Why? sound. The interplay with syncopation in beats as well as these moments of silence is something I have always loved about your music but it feels even more apparent on this new album. I know these sorts of stylistic choices aren't always conscious or intentional but I wonder about the Why? songwriting process in terms of the interplay between music and lyrics. Do these get written in a certain order (lyrics first, music later, or whatever)? How aware are you of these elements when you write? Is there ever a kind of awkwardness in marrying the two, as your lyrics and music both play around a lot with sound vs. silence?
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > I suppose for the most part I tend to write lyrics first and the go where they lead me but there is always extra lyric shaping once music is in process as well. They have to play off eachother a bit.
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > >   
> > > > 
> > > > After releasing albums every handful of years, there was an EP followed closely by an album this year. What prompted that decision and the order of the releases (instead of, say, an EP this year and an album next, or vice versa, or…..)?
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > The way that things ended up being released was strictly a question of logistics. It had to do with available times on record label schedules, etc.
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > >   
> > > > 
> > > > What has been the response to the album and EP? The songs were written in the same timeframe but do fans draw some kind of distinguishing line between them, or have they melded together as a cohesive kind of unit in the past couple months that you've been performing them on the road?
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > I'm not really sure what listeners make of the difference between the two but in terms of our playing, we have tended to play more of the LP songs live. Though we occasionally throw an EP song in the set now and again.
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > >   
> > > > 
> > > > Tell me about this Christmas song you have… is this a cover or an original? When is it due out? Will it be a single?
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > It's an origional. I wrote it for my (ex) girlfriend for Christmas a few years ago and then we recorded it during the Mumps sessions. I think it's pretty cool.
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > >   
> > > > 
> > > > I read that you hardly read (books anyway), which I think is interesting, because as a writer, I find that reading is what inspires me and grows my ideas, vocabulary, and approach to language. For someone like yourself who writes these lyrics that feel very intuitive yet not simplistic, is that natural, or do you have some kind of external influence (like reading works for many writers) that you find has an effect on your processes, ideas, etc.? Or would you perhaps attribute your lyrical outlet to being constantly engaged with words, ideas, occurrences, and writing them down when they happen as opposed to trying to drudge up material outside of a moment? (Sorry if it feels like I am going on here, but this process is of particular interest to me.)
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > I do stay on the ready to record/write an idea at any moment. That said, I also have a pretty good emotional/sensory memory and a vivid imagination so I can usually fill things in pretty well when necessary.
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > >   
> > > > 
> > > > Over the years, Why? has had various members but you have remained a constant, and many people still refer to it as your solo project, particularly since a large percentage of the music is distinctly you. How do you view things?
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > Every album has a different process. Mumps is very me I think because of the way it came about.

> > >

> > >   
> > > 
> > > >   
> > > > 
> > > > Additionally, how do your live shows vary from the band on the album? Do you bring additional members? Are there ways you deal with filling up the space or communicating the musical ideas from older songs with less or different instruments? Is that translation from recording to live song ever a difficult one to make?
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > We are traveling as a 6 piece. We make the arrangements we can make depending on the size of band/instrumentation.

> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > >   
> > > > 
> > > > What are the hardest or most challenging aspects of what you do? What are the most rewarding?
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > >   
> > > 
> > > It's all about managing the psyche. It's a strange strage job that can get to you mentally if you let it. You have to try to stay grounded and mellow.
